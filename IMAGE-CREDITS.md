# Image credits

Every photograph in `src/assets/images/` (and the two in `public/_assets/`:
`og-image.jpg` and `bg-texture.jpg`) is a real, licensed photograph from
**[Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/)**, downloaded on **2026-09-03** and
cropped to the slot dimensions listed below. No image is AI-generated.
The panorama was re-downloaded at full resolution on **2026-10-16** (same
photo, same framing) so the 4×-wide hero is no longer upscaled.

The previous release shipped 34 random placeholders from Lorem Picsum with no
recorded provenance, whose subjects contradicted their captions (the "Amazon"
card was a suspension bridge over a coastal bay, the "Borneo" card was an
aurora, and two files were the same photograph). **All of them have been
deleted.** Nothing in this table is a placeholder.

## Licence

All files below are covered by the **Pexels Licence**
(<https://www.pexels.com/license/>): free for commercial use, no attribution
required, modification allowed. The MIT License on the code does **not**
extend to these photographs. Two of the Pexels restrictions matter to you as
someone building on this template or as a site owner:

1. **You may not redistribute the photographs as standalone stock files.** They
   may ship as part of this template; they may not be extracted and resold as a
   photo pack.
2. **You may not imply that identifiable people in a photo endorse a product or
   service.** This is why every photograph here that contains a person shows
   them from behind, at distance, or hooded — nobody in this template is
   identifiable, and no photograph is paired with a testimonial or biography
   that would put words in a real person's mouth. **If you replace these with
   photographs of identifiable people, you need a signed model release, not just
   a photo licence.**

Each row's **Photo page** link is the canonical Pexels page: open it to see the
photographer's name and the current licence terms. The **file URL** pattern used
for every download was:

```
https://images.pexels.com/photos/<ID>/pexels-photo-<ID>.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600
```

**Why no faces.** These four slots sit beside invented names, nationalities and
biographies (`src/content/guides/*.json`). A photo licence does not grant the
right to attach a real, recognisable person to a fabricated biography — that is
a personality-rights and false-endorsement exposure regardless of the licence,
and the Pexels Licence forbids implying endorsement outright. Every figure here
is turned away, distant or hooded, so no individual is identifiable. **When you
launch, replace these with photographs of your actual guides, taken with their
written permission.**

## Other third-party assets

- **Archivo** and **Lora** are self-hosted from `src/assets/fonts/` (latin
  subset, originally from Google Fonts; `@font-face` rules in
  `src/styles/global.css`). The shipped files are static weights instanced
  from the variable fonts with fontTools; the variable originals are kept in
  `src/assets/fonts/variable/`. Both families are licensed under the **SIL Open
  Font License 1.1**, which allows modified versions such as these instances,
  and is free for commercial use and redistribution with the template.
- The grain overlay and crosshatch map grid are generated in CSS (`.grain`,
  `.mapGrid` in `src/styles/global.css`) — an inline SVG `feTurbulence` filter
  and two linear gradients. No third-party asset, nothing to attribute.
- The compass, arrow and checkmark icons are hand-written inline SVG inside the
  components. No icon library is bundled.
- **Gear-partner names are fictional placeholders.** Scarnwell, Halden Co-op,
  Northbound Atlas Expeditions, Corriloft, Granite Hollow, Talvik, Ferrow,
  Rook, Lumek, Drift-a-Loft, Skarre and Orvane in
  `src/components/PartnersMarquee.tsx` were invented for this demo; they are
  not real companies and imply no partnership. They stand in for **your own
  partners**: put your real partner names in that list (or remove the section
  if you have none). Like any demo copy, they were not cleared against
  trademark registers, which is one more reason to use your own.

## Replacing an image

Overwrite the file in `src/assets/images/` keeping its filename. No code
changes are needed: the build resizes it and emits WebP/AVIF variants
(`src/lib/images.ts`). A file name that is referenced but missing fails the
build with its name. Then **replace its row above**: filename, source URL,
photographer, licence and download date. That record is the only thing that
helps you in a takedown or an invoice dispute.

Recommended source sizes (larger is fine; the build never upscales):
